What is PCOS?

A common hormonal condition, and a very manageable one

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a hormonal condition that affects how the ovaries work. It can change your periods, your skin and hair, your weight and how your body uses insulin.

  • It is not your fault and not a sign you did something wrong.
  • It shows up differently in every woman, so symptoms can be mild or more noticeable.
  • Early awareness helps. With the right care, most women live healthy, full lives with PCOS.
1. Irregular or missed periodsOvulation may happen less often, so cycles can be long, unpredictable or absent.
2. Higher androgen ("male") hormonesThis can show up as extra hair growth, acne, or thinning scalp hair.
3. Ovaries that look polycysticMany small follicles on an ultrasound. Not everyone with PCOS has this, and "cysts" are not harmful tumours.
Know the signs

What PCOS can look like

Having one of these doesn't mean you have PCOS, and you don't need all of them. Notice patterns and share them with a doctor.

Irregular periods

Cycles shorter than 21 or longer than 35 days, or fewer than 9 periods a year.

Persistent acne

Breakouts along the jaw, chin and lower face that keep returning in adulthood.

Extra hair growth

Darker, coarser hair on the face, chest, tummy or back (called hirsutism).

Thinning scalp hair

Widening parting or thinning at the crown and temples.

Weight changes

Gaining weight easily, especially around the waist, or finding it hard to lose. PCOS also affects lean women.

Dark, velvety patches

Darker skin on the neck, underarms or groin can be a sign of insulin resistance.

Mood changes, tiredness and trouble sleeping are also common. They matter too.
